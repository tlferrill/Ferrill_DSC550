Rescaling

Both Pandas and NumPy are powerful libraries for handling and manipulating numerical data - especially when rescaling.  Also known as normalization/standardization, rescaling is a crutial preprocessing step for many data analysis and machine learning tasks.  

Rescaling includes different techniques, including min-max scaling (normalization), standardization (Z-score normalization), and max absolute scaling.  Each differ in their forumals, the range they produce, and their sensitivity to outliers.  

Min-max scaling rescales the data to a fixed range, typically [0,1], and preserves the original distribution.  It is best to use with data that has a known fixed range.  Min-max scaling is highly sensitive to outliers, extreme values can disproportionately compress the range of the rest of the data

Z-score normalization rescales the data to have a mean of 0 and a standard deviation of 1.  The range is not bounded.  Z-score transfors the data to a standard normal distribution.  It is best to use with data that follows a normal or near-normal distribution.  Z-score normalization is less affected by outliers than min-max scaling because it uses the standard deviation - be can be influenced by extremely distant outliers.

Max absolute scaling rescales the data to be within the rand of [-1,1], and preserves the sparsity of the data (the presence of zero values) - it does not center the data.  It is best to use with a sparce data set, when the dataset contains many zero values.  Max absolute scaling is highly sensitive to outliers because the scaling factor is the single maximum absolute value.

Choosing the appropriate scaling method depends on data characteristics along with the requirements of the machine learning model.
